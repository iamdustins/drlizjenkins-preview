ADHD & Sensory Differences: Understanding & Mutual Repair

Balanced Empathy

Neurological explanations foster compassion, but they do not dismiss emotional hurt or replace practical accountability. See how both partners work together.

The Situation:

Partner B forgot an urgent promised phone call; Partner A felt abandoned and exploded in frustration.

Partner A's Valid Experience

Partner A carries ongoing mental load and feels alone managing family logistics. When a promised call is dropped, it registers as emotional abandonment and disrespect, not just a minor slip.

Key Need: Felt reliability, genuine acknowledgment of the frustration, and concrete support.
Partner B's Neurological Reality

Working memory dropped the task during transition. It was executive dysfunction, not malicious intent or lack of love. However, using ADHD as an excuse without accountability creates resentment.

Key Need: Reassurance that they aren't broken, combined with external scaffolded routines.
The Mutual Repair Plan (Two-Way Accountability) Dr. Liz's Method
Partner A's Script (Connecting Without Contempt):

"I know you didn't deliberately forget, but I'm feeling deeply overwhelmed carrying this today. I need to know we're in this together."

Partner B's Script & Repair Action (Ownership & System):

"You're right to feel exhausted—leaving you hanging wasn't okay. I am setting a phone alarm right now, and let me take over the evening routine so you can rest."

Notice: Empathy explains the breakdown; practical action repairs the relationship.

"You’re Not Broken. You’re Just Out of Sync."

"I have sat across from thousands of couples over thirty-eight years. Couples who arrived in silence, clutching their coffee cups like shields, convinced that the love they once felt had died quietly in its sleep.

Here is what I tell them on day one: Love does not die quietly. It gets buried under the laundry, the unanswered texts, the defensiveness of exhaustion, and the silent assumption that our partner can read our minds.
